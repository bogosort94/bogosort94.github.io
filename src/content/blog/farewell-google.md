My time at Google and on the Spanner team has come to an end this month.

Looking back at my six years here, I'm deeply grateful for the chance to work on one of the world's most sophisticated and impactful systems. But besides the tech, I've been incredibly lucky to work alongside some of the brightest minds I've ever known. Their guidance has shaped how I think about building systems, balancing speed with quality, collaborating effectively, and knowing when to agree to disagree, and it's why I've become the engineer I am today.

For my next chapter, I'm moving back to China to be closer to my family, friends, and (very importantly) the fantastic food. I'll be settling in Chengdu, and my plan is to start something new and fun next year. While the specifics are still open, the direction is clear: follow my curiosity. We are living through dramatic changes that redefine access to knowledge and boost personal productivity. I am excited to see how these shifts will enable me to pursue all of my interests and passions.

Six years ago I joined a team keeping a database consistent across continents. Whatever I build next will be much smaller, much messier, and entirely mine to get wrong. After six years of not knowing what that feels like, that's the part I'm looking forward to.

I'll be writing it down here as it takes shape. If you travel to Chengdu, or you're working on something adjacent, I'd love to hear from you.
